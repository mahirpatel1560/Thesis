## 1. Business

Micron sells memory and storage products — DRAM, NAND, and NOR — under the Micron and Crucial brands, delivered as components, modules, SSDs, managed NAND, multi-chip packages, and wafers [10-K 1]. DRAM was $28.58B of FY2025 revenue, NAND $8.50B [10-K 1]. Products go to hyperscale cloud, data center, mobile, PC, automotive, industrial, and consumer markets [10-K 1].

The company reorganized into four segments in Q4 FY2025 [10-K 1]. FY2025 revenue by unit [10-K 7]:
- Cloud Memory (CMBU): $13.52B (36%) — HBM plus DDR/LPDDR/GDDR for data centers [10-K 7]
- Mobile & Client (MCBU): $11.86B (32%) [10-K 7]
- Core Data Center (CDBU): $7.23B (19%) [10-K 7]
- Automotive & Embedded (AEBU): $4.75B (13%) [10-K 7]

Roughly one-half of total revenue came from its top ten customers in each of the last three years [10-K 1]. About 80% of FY2025 revenue was from products shipped to customer locations outside the U.S.; about one-third was from customers headquartered outside the U.S. [10-K 1A]. Micron manufactures at wholly-owned facilities plus subcontractors, with a majority of FY2025 DRAM production output coming from Taiwan fabs [10-K 1A].

## 2. Moat

The packet points to a cost-and-technology position rather than a stated durable moat. Micron says it invests to increase bit density per wafer and reduce per-bit manufacturing cost each generation [10-K 1]. It reports process-technology milestones: first 1γ (1-gamma) DRAM node using EUV in 2025, HBM3E 12-high as the majority of HBM shipments in Q4 FY2025, and HBM4 36GB samples delivered to key customers [10-K 1]. Vertical integration in data center SSDs (controller, firmware, NAND, DRAM designed in-house) is described as a differentiator [10-K 1].

Against this, the company describes its industry as "highly competitive" with intense competition from Samsung, SK hynix, Kioxia, Sandisk, CXMT, and YMTC, and states competitors "may use aggressive pricing to obtain market share" [10-K 1A]. Customers are "generally reluctant to enter into long-term, fixed-price purchase contracts," and pricing is periodically renegotiated to market conditions [10-K 1]. DRAM average selling prices have swung from roughly +low-40% to −high-40% annually over the past five years [10-K 1A]. This is a commodity-cyclical structure; any advantage rests on being a low-cost, leading-node producer rather than on pricing power or switching costs.

## 4. Risks

Translated from Item 1A [10-K 1A]:

- **Price volatility.** Selling prices swing violently — DRAM ASPs have ranged roughly +40% to −40% in a year, NAND +30% to −50% — and at times have been below manufacturing cost [10-K 1A]. This is the central risk.
- **Gross margin pressure.** A large share of manufacturing cost is fixed; when fabs run underutilized (weak demand, oversupply, ramp delays), per-gigabit cost rises and margins fall [10-K 1A]. Excess/obsolete inventory can trigger write-downs (a $1.83B write-down hit FY2023) [10-K 7].
- **Oversupply from subsidized competitors.** Government-backed Chinese entrants CXMT and YMTC could add supply and depress prices [10-K 1A].
- **China / geopolitics.** The May 2023 CAC ruling bars critical-infrastructure operators in China from buying Micron products, hurting China revenue [10-K 1A]. A majority of DRAM output is in Taiwan, so any Taiwan disruption is material [10-K 1A].
- **Technology execution.** Must keep shrinking nodes (EUV, more 3D layers, HBM stacking) and win designs years ahead; failure to hit customer schedules risks exclusion as a supplier [10-K 1A].
- **Capacity-expansion returns.** Large U.S. fab builds (Idaho, New York) may not deliver expected returns; incentives are conditional and subject to clawback [10-K 1A].
- **AI demand uncertainty.** The company explicitly warns AI demand is new, evolving, and hard to forecast; it may spend ahead of demand that under-delivers [10-K 1A].
- **Financing / debt.** Capital intensity requires ongoing access to cash flow and external financing; debt obligations and buyback/dividend levels can fluctuate [10-K 1A].

## 6. Bull case / Bear case

**Bull (steelman):** AI demand is "accelerating and outpacing industry supply," per management, driving DRAM pricing, volume, and margin gains [10-K 7]. CMBU revenue grew 257% to $13.52B with a 45% operating margin, and HBM has moved to 12-high with HBM4 sampling [10-K 7][10-K 1]. Company-wide gross margin expanded from 22% to 40% in one year [10-K 7], operating cash flow doubled to $17.53B [10-K 7], and forward P/E of 6.0 [yf valuation] reflects expected continued earnings growth. Government support ($6.4B CHIPS grants, 35% investment tax credit, up to $5.5B New York funding) underpins U.S. capacity [10-K 7]. Headlines suggest analysts see limited threat from China's CXMT ("Wall Street Sees Little Threat to Micron From China's Memory Giant" [news: Wall Street Sees Little Threat to Micron From China's Memory Giant]).

**Bear (steelman):** This is a commodity cycle. ASPs have historically fallen as much as 40% (DRAM) and 50% (NAND) year over year [10-K 1A], and Micron has sold below cost before, taking a $1.83B inventory write-down in FY2023 and posting a −$5.83B net loss [10-K 7][yf financials]. Subsidized Chinese entrants CXMT and YMTC threaten oversupply [10-K 1A] — and CXMT's IPO "Surged 466%" [news: Micron's New Chinese Rival CXMT Surged 466% After IPO. What It Means for the Stock.]. HBM capacity could shift to conventional DRAM and flood that market [10-K 1A]. The stock is up 739.9% in a year [yf prices], and management itself warns AI demand may be spent ahead of and fail to materialize [10-K 1A]. FY2025 free cash flow was only $1.67B against $15.86B capex [yf financials][10-K 7], with heavy multi-year fab spending ahead.

## 7. What would kill this thesis

- **ASP reversal.** DRAM or NAND average selling prices turning negative year-over-year — the range extends to −40% (DRAM) / −50% (NAND) [10-K 1A]. Watch quarterly DRAM ASP commentary versus FY2025's +low-40% [10-K 7].
- **Gross margin rollover.** Consolidated gross margin falling back toward the 22% (FY2024) or negative (FY2023) levels from the 40% FY2025 print [10-K 7].
- **Inventory write-downs.** Any renewed NRV write-down (there was $1.83B in FY2023; $0 in FY2024–FY2025) would signal oversupply/underutilization [10-K 7].
- **CXMT/YMTC supply.** Evidence of large-scale Chinese DRAM/NAND capacity coming online at aggressive pricing [10-K 1A][news: How CXMT Stacks Up Against Memory-Chip Rivals After Blockbuster IPO].
- **China actions.** Further CAC or Chinese-government restrictions beyond the May 2023 ban [10-K 1A].
- **Taiwan disruption.** Any political/economic event affecting Taiwan, source of a majority of DRAM output [10-K 1A].
- **Fab-ramp slippage / incentive clawback.** Delays to Idaho (first DRAM output projected 2H calendar 2027) or New York, or reduction/clawback of the up-to-$6.4B CHIPS grants [10-K 7].
- **AI demand shortfall.** Deceleration in the hyperscaler/HBM demand that drove CMBU's 257% growth — management flags this demand as hard to forecast [10-K 1A][10-K 7].

## 8. Verdict

This is a deep-cyclical memory maker at a cyclical high — revenue up 49%, gross margin at 40% [10-K 7], and the stock up ~740% in a year [yf prices] — where the debate hinges on whether AI-driven DRAM/HBM demand sustains pricing or whether the historical ASP down-cycle (and subsidized Chinese supply) reasserts, a tension the 6.0x forward P/E versus 20.8x trailing P/E [yf valuation] frames directly.
