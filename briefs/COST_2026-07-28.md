## 1. Business

Costco operates membership warehouses and e-commerce sites, selling a limited selection of branded and private-label products across a wide range of categories at low gross margins, relying on high sales volume and rapid inventory turnover [10-K 1]. It operated 914 warehouses worldwide at August 31, 2025 (up from 890 and 861 in the prior two years) [10-K 1]. The core concept is carrying fewer than 4,000 active SKUs per warehouse in the core business — far fewer than broadline retailers — to drive volume purchasing efficiencies [10-K 1].

Revenue comes from two streams: merchandise sales and membership fees [10-K 7]. Net sales were $269,912M in FY2025, and membership fee revenue was $5,323M, up 10% [10-K 7]. The annual U.S. membership fee is $65, with an Executive upgrade for an additional $65 that earns a 2% reward (capped generally at $1,250/year) [10-K 1]. Total paid members reached 81,000 thousand in FY2025, of which 38,700 thousand were Executive members; Executive members represented approximately 73.6% of worldwide net sales [10-K 1].

Product categories are Core Merchandise (Foods and Sundries, Non-Foods, Fresh Foods), Warehouse Ancillary (gasoline, pharmacy, optical, food court, etc.), and Other Businesses (e-commerce, business centers, travel) [10-K 1]. Gasoline was approximately 10% of total net sales, and e-commerce approximately 7%, in FY2025 [10-K 1]. The company employed 341,000 people worldwide at year-end 2025 [10-K 1].

## 2. Moat

The membership model is the structural advantage: recurring fee revenue and a loyalty mechanism [10-K 1]. The U.S./Canada renewal rate was 92.3% and the worldwide rate 89.8% at the end of 2025 [10-K 1]. Membership fee revenue of $5,323M [10-K 7] compares to net income of $8,099M [10-K 7] — meaning fees supply a large share of pre-tax profit, a recurring base that merchandise margins alone do not.

Scale purchasing lets Costco operate profitably at "significantly lower gross margins than most other retailers" [10-K 1]. FY2025 gross margin percentage was 11.12% [10-K 7], reflecting that low-margin/high-volume model. The Kirkland Signature private label is a differentiator that "generally earn[s] higher margins" and drives loyalty [10-K 1], and one recent headline frames it as a growth engine [news: Costco's Kirkland Brand Is Becoming a Powerful Growth Engine]. The company also cites low inventory shrinkage from the controlled-entry membership format [10-K 1].

The honest limit: the company itself states its industry is "highly competitive" and that some competitors have greater financial resources, technology capabilities, faster AI adoption, and greater market penetration [10-K 1A]. Named competitors include Walmart, Target, Kroger, Amazon, Sam's Club, and BJ's [10-K 1].

## 4. Risks

Translated from Item 1A [10-K 1A]:

- **Geographic concentration.** U.S. and Canada were 86% of net sales and 84% of operating income in 2025, and California alone was 26% of U.S. net sales [10-K 1A]. A slowdown in these regions — or a California-specific event — hits results disproportionately [10-K 1A].
- **Growth execution.** New warehouses can cannibalize existing ones, new markets may not turn profitable, and suitable sites/permits are increasingly hard to secure [10-K 1A].
- **Membership dependence.** Profitability hinges on renewal rates and Executive penetration; brand or reputation damage would reduce renewals and fee revenue [10-K 1A]. Renewal rates are being pressured downward by online-sold memberships, which renew at slightly lower rates [10-K 7].
- **Kirkland brand risk.** A loss of confidence in the private label would hurt sales and margin, since these products carry higher margins [10-K 1A].
- **Competition and technology.** Larger competitors with more resources and faster AI adoption could take market share [10-K 1A].
- **Tariffs and macro.** Trade actions have already affected merchandise costs; "higher tariffs are more likely to adversely impact rather than improve our results" [10-K 7]. Inflation, unemployment, and consumer confidence all affect demand [10-K 1A].
- **Foreign exchange.** International operations were 27% of net sales and 34% of operating income in 2025; FX cut FY2025 net income by $97M ($0.22/share) [10-K 1A][10-K 7].
- **Cybersecurity / data / payments.** High transaction volume and sensitive data make IT failure or breach a material threat to operations and member confidence [10-K 1A].
- **Product safety.** Food and consumer goods carry illness/injury and recall risk [10-K 1A].
- **Catastrophe concentration.** Centralized systems and administration in California and Washington create disaster exposure [10-K 1A].

## 7. What would kill this thesis

Checkable triggers:
- **Renewal rate breaks lower.** U.S./Canada renewal was 92.3% and worldwide 89.8% at end of FY2025 [10-K 1]; a sustained decline below these levels in future filings would undercut the fee-annuity argument.
- **Comparable sales stall.** FY2025 total-company comp was 6% (8% ex-FX/gasoline) [10-K 7]; a drop toward flat or negative comps, especially in the U.S. (6% in FY2025), would signal traffic erosion.
- **Membership fee revenue growth reverses.** FY2025 fee revenue was $5,323M, +10% [10-K 7]; note ~40% of that growth came from the September 1, 2024 fee increase [10-K 7], so watch whether organic sign-ups sustain growth after the fee-hike benefit laps.
- **Margin compression.** Gross margin percentage was 11.12% and SG&A 9.25% of net sales [10-K 7]; a reversal in gross margin or SG&A deleverage from wage/healthcare/tariff costs would pressure the thin 2.9% net margin [yf financials].
- **California / concentration shock.** Any material disruption to California (26% of U.S. sales) or to the Washington/California centralized operations [10-K 1A].
- **Warehouse-opening shortfall.** Plan is up to 35 new warehouses (five relocations) in FY2026 [10-K 7]; materially missing this pace would slow the square-footage growth lever.
- **Dated event.** Q4 FY2026 earnings — a recent headline previews it [news: What to Expect From Costco Wholesale's Q4 2026 Earnings Report] — is the next observable checkpoint for comps, renewal, and fee growth.
